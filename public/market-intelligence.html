Seasonality
Seasonal Price Patterns
Average month-by-month price relative to the year's average (100 = the annual average). Built from 15 years of Nebraska cash prices. Patterns move with the cattle cycle and your region, so treat them as a guide, not a forecast.
Calves, 500-600 lb steers: Nebraska 2001-2015 ran about 3% above the year's average in June through August and about 2.5% below in October and November, when the fall calf run hits. Arkansas 2011-2020 found the high in March and prices below average through the fall. Both agree on the fall: that is the low for calves.
Yearling feeders, 700-800 lb steers: about 5% above average July through September and about 6% below in January and February (Nebraska 2001-2015). Heavier feeders follow the annual average more closely than calves (Arkansas).
Fed steers, live: strongest in March and April, about 2 to 3% above average, and weakest in June and July, about 3% below (Nebraska 2001-2015).
Cull cows: we have not sourced a cull cow index yet, so there is no cull cow curve on this chart.
Source: Birch and Brooks, Nebraska Historical Prices, Seasonal Price Patterns, and Futures Basis, Beef Cattle 2001-2015 (University of Nebraska-Lincoln Extension EC291), 15-year monthly indexes, Tables 2, 4 and 19. Arkansas statements from Mitchell, Feeder Cattle Price Seasonality 2011-2020 (University of Arkansas FSA3159).
